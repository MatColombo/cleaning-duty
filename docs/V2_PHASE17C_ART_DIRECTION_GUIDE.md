# House Care V2 — Phase 17C Compact Art Direction Guide

## Intent
Aim for **70% modern readability / 30% retro character**.
The reference mood is **mid-century advertising / screenprint mascot art for adults**, not toys or children’s media.

## 1) Character language
- Prefer **big simple shapes** over many tiny internal details.
- Faces must read clearly at **48–96 px**.
- Eyebrows, eyes and mouths should carry the emotion quickly.
- Keep silhouettes broad and recognizable.

## 2) Hands and poses
- Use rubber-hose-inspired arms with obvious glove cuffs.
- A positive hand should read as **clearly thumbs-up**, never as a single raised finger.
- When in doubt, favor open-hand or presentational poses over ambiguous micro-detail.

## 3) Composition
- Front cards should feel like **small posters**.
- Establish a clear focal order:
  1. main hero subject
  2. expressive face / gesture
  3. supporting tool or scene prop
  4. secondary decorations
- Scene props should stay simple and iconic.

## 4) Print treatment
- Keep to a **small 3–5 ink feeling**.
- Use halftone / misregistration subtly as flavor, not noise.
- Avoid dense texture behind metrics, labels or charts.

## 5) Motion
- Motion is **finite, occasional and local**.
- Good motions: tiny billboard tilt, short blink, short hand emphasis.
- Bad motions: continuous idle loops, drifting turbulence, heavy per-card animation.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and offscreen guards.

## 6) Accessibility guardrails
- Decorative art must not be the only place where state is communicated.
- Buttons, text labels and keyboard behavior remain the functional truth.
- Contrast and readability always beat decorative density.

## 7) Avoid
- Childlike proportions or “cute toy” expressions
- Generic corporate vector flatness
- Over-texturing the data region
- Runtime AI/image generation
- Storing art externally or generating raster card exports at runtime
